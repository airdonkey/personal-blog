I am Zhou Penglong, a writer and educator based in New Zealand.

These identities do not form a tidy professional label, and I do not want to present myself as someone who has mastered every field he has entered. What connects them is a situation I have repeatedly faced: when a person without inherited status leaves a familiar environment, changes careers, and enters a new era, what can he rely on to build himself again?

## Where I came from

I came from an ordinary family in China. Like many people whose first reliable route out of a small town was education, my early forms of capital were study, examinations, and effort. They helped me leave the town, enter university, and reach Beijing and a wider professional world.

There I gradually saw the limits of effort. Talent and hard work are real, but they do not always produce equal outcomes when they meet overwhelming differences in resources, family background, and social networks.

I no longer romanticise effort, but I cannot dismiss it. For people without a ready-made safety net, it remains a force we can return to.

## The professional paths I have taken

After university, I began in audit at Deloitte Beijing, later worked in credit risk analysis at Sinosure, and contributed to work connected with the Export-Import Bank of China in Central Asia and the Transcaucasus. These roles exposed me to companies, financial institutions, national projects, and cooperation across different systems. They also showed me how large narratives eventually arrive in the specific lives of ordinary people.

In the mid-2010s, I left the familiar world of finance, moved to New Zealand, and entered education. Professional identity, language advantages, and social relationships were all repriced, and in many areas I had to begin again.

This was not a clean story of successful reinvention. Migration and career change involve loss of status, skills that no longer transfer, long periods of learning, and practical compromise. That is exactly why they became my most useful material for understanding how a life is rebuilt.

## What I am doing now

- I am a registered secondary teacher in New Zealand, observing how learning, culture, institutions, and technology shape young people in real classrooms.
- I serve as Executive Secretary of the Royal Astronomical Society of New Zealand and contribute to New Zealand astronomy olympiad work, learning how a professional community is sustained through public service and volunteer cooperation.
- I am studying toward a master's degree in applied computing. Returning to programming as a beginner after forty lets me experience directly how artificial intelligence is changing the entry barriers to learning and work.
- I maintain this website so that experiences and thoughts left unorganised for years can gradually become public work that compounds and can be tested by readers.

These activities can grow over time, but together they already form the ground of the writing.

## Why I write

I learned early that I did not want to build a life around competition for position and influence. I would rather build personal leverage through long-term learning, dependable work, and a body of public writing that does not depend entirely on one institution.

The internet gives people without established reputations a chance to find real readers through a body of work accumulated over time. This site is where I begin repaying a long-standing debt: thinking a great deal while leaving too little behind.

This site is not a display cabinet for unrelated credentials, nor does it offer ready-made success formulas. It follows how an unknown person meets unequal resources, migration, changing identities, career transitions, and technological change—and keeps trying to frame a new question for his own life.

My basic position is simple:

> Do not romanticise effort or mock it; do not deny structure or surrender yourself to it.

## What I intend to keep writing

- **[The work of rebuilding](/en/themes/#self-building)**: effort, unequal resources, ambition, family responsibility, and personal choice.
- **[Migration and being renamed](/en/themes/#migration)**: China, Central Asia, New Zealand, and identity in unfamiliar coordinates.
- **[Career change and lifelong learning](/en/themes/#work-and-learning)**: moving from finance to education, returning from competence to beginnerhood, and continuing to grow in midlife.
- **[Education, technology, and human scale](/en/themes/#education-and-technology)**: classrooms, artificial intelligence, astronomy, general education, and the parts of learning that tools cannot complete for us.

These are not rigid professional categories. They are four containers into which I can continue adding experience, stories, and judgement.

## Who I write for

I write for people without prestigious backgrounds who still want a life with a recognisable personal shape; for those learning, choosing, and rebuilding identity midway through life; and for those with rich inner lives who have not yet turned enough of them into work.

I do not expect every reader to like these essays. I hope a smaller number will recognise their atmosphere and return at different stages of their own lives.

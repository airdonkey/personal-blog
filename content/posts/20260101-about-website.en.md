This site serves as a dedicated space for me to document my personal observations and reflections.

## Through this platform, I aim to:

1. **Share insights on cross-cultural education**

   Having lived and worked across China, Central Asia, and New Zealand—transitioning from a career in finance to one in education—I have developed a unique vantage point to observe the strengths and limitations of various educational systems. I want to share the cross-cultural insights I've gathered along the way.

2. **Advocate for a liberal arts / general education approach**

   In today's increasingly specialized world, the value of a broad, general education is often overlooked. I want to explore how we can foster interdisciplinary thinking during a student's formative years.

3. **Integrate astronomy into the classroom**

   Astronomy is more than just a science; for those of us drawn to the stars, it is an incredible vehicle for sparking curiosity and imagination. I want to explore ways to weave astronomy into primary and secondary school curricula.

4. **Delve into educational history and comparative education**

   My pivot from finance to education was driven by a deep curiosity about a fundamental question: What is education? I am not satisfied with merely knowing how to teach; I want to understand why we teach the way we do.

   The history of education reveals that the paradigms we take for granted today did not appear out of nowhere. From Comenius to Dewey, from Montessori to Freire, every educational thinker was responding to the unique challenges of their time. Tracing these historical threads helps us view our current teaching practices with a more critical eye.

   Comparative education, meanwhile, satisfies my cross-cultural curiosity. My experiences in China, Central Asia, and New Zealand have taught me one crucial lesson: no educational system is perfect. Each is deeply rooted in its own cultural soil. My goal isn't to debate which system is "better," but rather to ask, "What can we learn from one another?"

5. **Document an ordinary journey of lifelong learning**

   From a small-town boy focused on passing exams, to a finance professional, and ultimately an educator, my path might offer some encouragement to others: it is never too late to change course, and learning is a lifelong endeavor.

## Connecting with Fellow Educators

If you are a frontline teacher—whether in China or abroad—with an interest in the history of education, comparative education, or cross-cultural teaching, I would love to connect. I firmly believe that the most valuable educational insights come from practitioners in the trenches, rather than theorists in ivory towers.

I am looking to exchange ideas with peers who are doing the daily work in the classroom. I want to hear about the dilemmas you face, your observations on the educational system, and the wisdom you've gained from your students. These authentic, on-the-ground experiences are the true keys to understanding the essence of education.

Regardless of where you are in the world, teaching is rarely the most lucrative career choice. Because of that, I know that if you are still spending your limited free time exploring the deeper philosophies of education, you must share my genuine passion for this profession.

It is a pleasure to meet you.

We can discuss:

- How do the differences between Eastern and Western educational philosophies actually play out in the classroom?
- How do teachers in different countries tackle similar educational challenges?
- Do classic theories from educational history still hold up in today's classrooms?
- As teachers, how do we stay true to our educational ideals while navigating institutional constraints?

When I first built this site, I described it as a place to write about cross-cultural education, general education and astronomy. Those interests remain real. But the description now feels too much like a list of topics, and too narrow for the question that keeps bringing me back to writing.

My own route has not been linear. I moved from a small town to Beijing through the examination system, worked in audit, credit risk and banking, spent time in Central Asia, migrated to New Zealand and retrained as a teacher. In my forties, I became a computing student and a beginner again. At each transition, some part of the rulebook that had previously guided me stopped working.

Over time, one question has connected these different lives: when institutions, identities and rules change, how does an ordinary person without a large reserve of inherited resources recover room to act?

## The questions behind the topics

I want to examine how education changes the trajectory of a family, and why the route that helped one generation move upward may not work in the same way for the next. I want to write about migration not only as opportunity, but also as the loss and reconstruction of identity. I want to look at career change and adult learning without turning them into inspirational slogans, including their costs, uncertainty and embarrassment. I also want to ask what technology should take over from us—and what judgment and responsibility should remain human.

Education, finance, classrooms, books, astronomy, travel and artificial intelligence will all appear here. They are not separate brands. They are different places from which I can examine institutions, risk, learning and human agency.

## Why keep an independent site

Social platforms are useful for helping a piece of writing travel. They are less useful for preserving the route by which a body of thought develops. Older work disappears down the feed, and a current opinion is easily separated from the experiences and revisions that produced it.

I want this site to be a durable archive rather than an expanded CV or a content treadmill. A question can be revisited more than once. A memory can begin as a sketch and gain detail later. An essay can change when better evidence or experience changes my judgment.

## The standard I want to use

My experience across countries, occupations and education systems gives me first-hand material, but personal experience is not proof that the world works in the same way for everyone. When I make a broader claim, I will try to distinguish memory, verifiable evidence and a hypothesis that is still being tested. I also want to leave room for counterexamples and limits.

Not every piece will wait until it is beyond revision. Some pages will be clearly marked as work in progress. That is partly practical: for me, a meaningful draft that exists is easier to improve than a perfect article that remains in my head. A public draft should still tell the reader what it already establishes and what remains unresolved.

The site's [essays](/en/posts/) develop fuller arguments. [Memoir](/en/memoir/) preserves the history of family, education, work and migration. [Side Paths](/en/notes/) holds smaller observations before they need to become large claims. [Grand Tour](/en/grand-tour/) returns to places through routes, photographs and later reflection. The forms differ, but the underlying questions should gradually meet.

## Who I hope will return

I do not expect every reader to care about every subject. I am writing especially for people whose old map no longer quite matches the world: migrants, career changers, parents trying to decide which parts of their own success to pass on, and adults willing to become beginners again. They may not want another universal formula, but they have not given up looking for a workable next step.

If someone first arrives through an essay on education, returns because a migration story feels familiar, and later becomes willing to follow me into a question they had not expected to care about, the site will have developed real continuity.

## A sustainable rhythm

I plan to publish one core piece each week, usually on Saturday in Auckland. Essays, memoir and travel records will take turns; shorter notes will appear only when there is something worth preserving. Chinese will remain the continuously developing source archive. English pieces will be selectively rewritten for a different readership rather than produced as a complete mirror.

Readers can return occasionally or subscribe through [RSS](/en/index.xml). I do not intend to manufacture a new opinion every day. The more modest promise is that each substantial update should move one of these long-term questions a little further.

Years from now, I hope this site will show not a person chasing unrelated subjects, but a set of questions becoming more precise through lived experience. I want to preserve experience as material, turn unease into questions that can be examined, and develop a few of those questions into judgments worth returning to.
